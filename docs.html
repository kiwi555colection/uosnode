01What UOS is

UOS is a bandwidth network. You install a lightweight node, it lends out the internet capacity your device isn't using, and you're paid in $UOS for the traffic it carries. That's the whole idea. Everything below is detail.

The name stands for Universal Orchestration System. "Universal" because almost any always-on device can take part — a laptop left open, a phone on the charger, a spare mini-PC. "Orchestration" because the hard part isn't collecting bandwidth, it's deciding which node should handle which request at any given moment. That routing layer is what makes the whole thing usable instead of just a pile of idle connections.

You don't buy hardware. You don't babysit anything. Once a node is connected, it works in the background and stops when you close it.

02How the network works

Four moving parts, in order:

The node layer

Your device. It advertises how much spare bandwidth it can offer and stays reachable while it's online. Nodes hold no special role beyond being available and honest about their capacity.

The orchestration layer

The coordinator. When there's demand for bandwidth in a region, the orchestrator picks nodes to serve it based on where they are, how reliable they've been, and how much headroom they have right now. It spreads load so no single node gets hammered and quiet nodes still get work. This is the part the project is named after, and it's the part that's genuinely hard to get right.

The verification layer

The referee. Every unit of work a node claims to have done is measured and checked before it counts. A node can't simply report "I was online for six hours, pay me" — the traffic it carried has to be attributable. This is what keeps rewards tied to real contribution instead of to whoever lies loudest.

The settlement layer

The cashier. Verified work turns into $UOS and lands on Robinhood Chain, at your payout address, on a regular schedule. Because payouts are small and frequent, this layer lives or dies on transaction cost — more on that below.

In one sentence

Nodes offer spare bandwidth → the orchestrator routes real demand to the right nodes → the work gets verified → you get paid in $UOS on Robinhood Chain.

03Getting started

Three steps, and the third one is the only one that keeps running:

  1. Connect X. Sign up with your X account from the button in the top bar. This becomes your identity on the network — no separate password to lose.
  2. Add a payout address. Open Profile and paste an EVM address (starts with 0x, 40 hex characters after it). This is where your $UOS is sent, so double-check it. You can change it later, but rewards always follow whatever's saved at payout time.
  3. Start your node. On the dashboard, hit Start node. Throughput, uptime, and pending rewards start moving. Leave it running.

That's it. Everything after this point is the node doing its job while you don't think about it.

04Running a node

A node is just your device running UOS in the background and keeping a live connection to the network. A few things worth knowing:

  • Uptime is the real currency. The orchestrator favours nodes it can count on. A node that's online eight hours straight will pick up more work than one that flickers on and off, even if the flaky one has faster internet.
  • It uses spare capacity, not all of it. The point is to share what you aren't using. Your own browsing, calls, and downloads come first; the node takes what's left.
  • Closing the app stops the node. No traffic is carried while it's off, and no rewards accrue. Nothing lingers in the background once you've quit.
  • One identity, multiple devices. You can point more than one device at the same account. Each one is its own node; they don't compete, they add up.

05Earning and claiming

Rewards build up as pending while your node works. The dashboard shows three numbers that matter:

  • Pending rewards — $UOS earned this session and any earlier sessions, not yet claimed.
  • Lifetime claimed — the running total you've moved out of pending.
  • Session earned — just this run, so you can see the rate in real time.

When you press Claim rewards, your pending balance is finalised and queued for settlement to your payout address on Robinhood Chain. Settlement is designed to be fast and cheap enough that even small balances are worth claiming.

On numbers

How much a node earns depends on uptime, available bandwidth, and how much demand exists near it — none of which is fixed. Anyone quoting you a guaranteed daily figure is guessing. Run a node, watch your own rate, decide from there. None of this is financial advice.

06Robinhood Chain

Rewards settle on Robinhood Chain, an EVM network. We picked it for one boring reason: cost. A bandwidth network pays out constantly and in tiny amounts, so if each payout burned a meaningful fee, the model would collapse under its own gas. Robinhood Chain keeps per-payout cost negligible and confirms in seconds, which is exactly what micro-rewards need.

07How work is verified

The uncomfortable question for any "share your resources and earn" network is: what stops people from faking it? UOS answers that in the verification layer.

Claims are never taken at face value. A node's reported contribution is cross-checked against the traffic that actually flowed and the demand it was actually assigned. Reliability is scored over time, so a node has to keep behaving to keep earning — a single good hour doesn't buy a reputation, and one bad stretch doesn't erase it. Nodes that consistently report more than they deliver simply get routed less work, which quietly starves the exploit instead of playing whack-a-mole with it.

The short version: rewards track verified contribution, not self-reported uptime. That distinction is the difference between a real network and a faucet people drain.

08Security and privacy

  • Your keys stay yours. UOS never asks for a seed phrase or private key. It only needs a public payout address. If anything ever asks you for your seed phrase, it isn't us — walk away.
  • You control the node. It runs when you start it and stops when you close it. Nothing persists in the background after you quit.
  • Sign-in is through X. Your account is tied to your X identity, not a password we store.
  • Do your own research. This is crypto and it's early. Only put in time and resources you're comfortable with, and treat every yield claim — including quiet ones — with healthy suspicion.

09FAQ

Do I need to buy anything?

No. No hardware, no license, no upfront token purchase. A device you already own and an internet connection are the whole kit.

Will this slow down my internet?

The node is meant to use idle capacity, so day-to-day it should stay out of your way. If you're doing something heavy and want it fully out of the picture, stop the node.

Can I run more than one device?

Yes. Point each device at the same account and they all count as separate nodes under one payout address.

Where does the money to pay nodes come from?

From the demand side — the parties that need bandwidth and pay for it. A supply-only network with no buyers isn't sustainable, which is why building real demand is the part that matters most. See the whitepaper for the economic model.

Is $UOS live and tradeable?

Check the current status in-app and on the official channels before assuming anything. The project is a work in progress and this page won't always be the freshest source.
